HOW TO PLAY

Marble Summit rules

The race

Each racer has four marbles in their starting base. Roll a 1 or 6 and choose a base marble to put on your start space. On future rolls, move a marble exactly the number of spaces on the die, clockwise around the outside. A 6 earns another roll if that option is enabled.

Blocking and bumping

You may pass an opponent's marble. Landing on one sends that marble to its base. You may never pass or land on your own marbles. Starting bases and home lanes are safe; the start space, shortcut rings, and center are not.

Finishing

Each player has four numbered home recesses. When you pass your own home entry on the outer track, turn into your home lane. The roll must place you in an empty home recess without passing another of your own marbles. First to safely fill all four home spaces wins.

Shortcuts (optional)

Land exactly on a golden star on the outside. On a later turn you may enter the first inner ring from that star and travel clockwise around its recesses. You can exit to the outside from any star's inner-ring position. Summit boards add a second, higher ring accessible from the first. Inner paths are shorter but shared and exposed to captures.

The center peak

Enter the center using the last exact step of a move from the innermost ring. Once inside, your marble is stuck until you roll the required exit number. The Classic and Triangle boards require a 1; Summit can be configured to accept a 1 or 6. That roll moves your marble to your choice of open inner-ring spaces and ends that marble's movement for the roll. You can be bumped off the center by another player.

Computer players

Computer racers can be Easy, Medium, or Hard. Each one can be Aggressive, Careful, Shortcut Hunter, Balanced, or Chaotic. Set the animation speed separately from difficulty. When two or more paths end at the same recess, choose the one you want from the move list.

Marble Summit is an independently made marble-racing game inspired by traditional marble races. The terraced Summit routes, AI, visuals, and some shortcut timing are original house rules.
