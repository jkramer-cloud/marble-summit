Quick Rules

A 1 or 6 releases a marble or opens an uphill shortcut. A 6 earns another roll. Land on another player's marble to send it to base. Don't pass your own marbles. Complete exact rolls to fill all four home spaces. Shortcuts are risky—opponents can capture marbles on the inner rings.

HOW TO PLAY

Marble Summit rules

The race

Each racer has four marbles in their starting base. Roll a 1 or 6 and choose a base marble to put on your start space. On future rolls, move a marble exactly the number of spaces on the die, clockwise around the outside. A 6 earns another roll if that option is enabled.

Blocking and bumping

You may pass an opponent's marble. Landing on one sends that marble to its base. You may never pass or land on your own marbles. Starting bases and home lanes are safe; the start space, shortcut rings, and center are not.

Finishing

Each player has four numbered home recesses. Your HOME ENTRY is on the right side of your own arm, one track space BEFORE your START. You must travel almost a complete lap to reach it. When you reach your HOME ENTRY hole, you can turn into your home lane. The roll must place you in an empty home recess without passing another of your own marbles. First to safely fill all four home spaces wins.

Shortcuts (optional)

Stop on the gold GATE star. On a later roll of 1 or 6, you may climb to the lower shortcut ring using the entire roll. On boards with 5 to 8 corners, a second roll of 1 or 6 lets you climb again to the three-station upper ring. You can travel clockwise on a ring, or descend back to a lower ring or the outside on a normal roll. All shortcut spaces are shared and can be captured.

The center shortcut

From the highest ring, roll a 1 or 6 to climb to the center, spending the entire roll. The center is a risky waiting space: you cannot move until you roll the designated exit number (1 normally; optional 1 or 6 on the larger polygon boards). On those larger boards, choose one of only THREE upper-ring exits. Captures still apply. Shortcut holes and the center are not safe from captures.

Computer players

Computer racers can be Easy, Medium, or Hard. Each one can be Aggressive, Careful, Shortcut Hunter, Balanced, or Chaotic. Set the animation speed separately from difficulty. When two or more paths end at the same recess, choose the one you want from the move list.

Marble Summit is an independently made marble-racing game inspired by traditional marble races. The expanded polygon boards, custom player options, and some shortcut timing are original house rules.
